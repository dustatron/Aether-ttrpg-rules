# Dying and Recovery

## Health, Scars, and recovery

Subtract damage from your character's current Health. Their Archetype sets the maximum. Health cannot fall below zero.

Reaching zero Health does not cause a Scar. When your character takes damage while already at zero Health, mark one Scar. A single hit causes only one Scar, regardless of how much damage it deals.

Each Scar occupies an inventory slot until removed. Restoring Health does not remove Scars.

### Fatigue

Mark 1 Fatigue when your character goes without proper rest or food, or consumes poison. Each Fatigue occupies one inventory slot.

### Healing

- **One quiet hour in a safe place:** Roll 2d6 and restore that much Health.
- **A full night's rest:** Remove all Fatigue and restore all Health.

To catch their breath, spend the character's action and roll **2d6 plus Willpower**.

| Total | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 6 or less | Restore no Health. The situation gets worse. After resolving the failure, gain 1 Luck. |
| 7 to 9 | Restore 1 Health. |
| 10 or greater | Restore 2 Health. |
| Double 1s | Restore no Health and resolve a Tragic failure. |
| Double 6s | Restore 3 Health. On the character's next turn, roll 3d6 and keep the highest two for their first roll. |

Health cannot exceed the character's maximum.

Removing a Scar requires [[Casting Magic#Magical healing|magic]] or about a week of care from a healer. Removing it frees its inventory slot.

## Death and dying

The condition that claims your character's final inventory slot determines their fate.

| Final condition | Ending |
| --- | --- |
| Mutation | The character becomes a Demon. The Aether has changed them beyond themselves. |
| Scar | The character's body dies. Their spirit passes on, or lingers because of unfinished business. |
| Fatigue | The character's body gives out from exhaustion. |

The character becomes part of the world's lore, and the player makes someone new. A lingering spirit may still matter, but the character's playable life is over.
