# Magic System

## Magical casting

Your character can command the patterns of Aether and reshape reality through thought and imagination.

1. Describe the effect and desired  magnitude. This can be total effect in size or duration or both.
2. Once the cost has been agreed on, then roll 2d6 plus one relevant Spell Tag.
3. If there was a success mark the exposure.

Casting follows the [[Core Mechanics#The dice|normal roll results]], with these additional effects:

- **Double 1s:** The cast fails spectacularly. Your character gains no Exposure. Roll d66 on the [[wild-magic-table|Wild Magic table]].
- **Double 6s:** The cast executes spectacularly. Mark the  Exposure and then gain, or improve a Spell Tag as described below.

A beneficial flourish to the cast can be added to the scene when it makes sense in the story.

### Spell Tags

Spell Tags represent what your character has learned through casting. Fire, invisibility, healing, ice, mind reading, floating, talking to animals, light, pain, and poison are all possible Tags.

Choose one relevant Spell Tag for each cast and add its rating to the roll. Tags do not stack, run out, reduce Exposure, or limit what your character may attempt. Without a relevant Tag, roll 2d6 with no bonus.

Casting does not use a Stat. Only one relevant Spell Tag can add to the roll.

### Learning Spell Tags

When your character attempts a cast without a relevant Spell Tag, write down a fitting Tag with an empty four-segment clock. An unfinished Tag adds no bonus to the roll.

After each successful cast that fits the Tag, fill one segment. Partial and full successes count. When the fourth segment is filled, erase the clock and record the Tag at +1.

When your character gains a Mutation through casting or you roll double 6s on a cast, choose one instead of filling a clock segment:

- Complete an unfinished Tag related to the cast at +1.
- Gain a new +1 Tag related to the cast.
- Increase the Tag used by 1, to a maximum of +3.

If both a Mutation and double 6s happen on the same cast, gain this benefit only once.

The [[Spell Tags]] table is a prompt sheet, not a spell list.

### Magic Magnitude

The magnitude should be, player facing, simple and fast at the table. Its cost should inform the players choice in how big they want their cast to be.

Over all the segments are  based on the spell's overall scale, power, reach, damage, and duration. But don’t sweat the small stuff, pick a number and keep the story moving.

| Magnitude | Exposure | Damage guideline | Example                                                        |
| --------- | -------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tiny      | 1        | 0                | Create a harmless spark on something the character touches.    |
| Small     | 2        | 1                | Give one nearby creature a firm magical shove.                 |
| Medium    | 4        | 2                | Fill a room with an illusion for a scene.                      |
| Large     | 6        | 3                | Hide a building or group of buildings behind an illusion.      |
| Huge      | 8        | 4                | Cover a field or city in magical fog.                          |
| Mythic    | 10       | 8                | Permanently transform a whole building into a living creature. |


For scrying, mind reading, and other information magic, consider how far away the subject is, what the character learns, and how long they observe. Discovery, misleading visions, or the subject sensing the intrusion may still be consequences when they fit.

Casting effects end when their agreed duration expires. A portal closes, a transformed person returns to their previous form, invisibility ends, and a flying boat comes back down.

### Magical healing

Medium magic handles simple injuries, normally restoring 2 Health. Large magic handles major wounds, normally restoring 3 Health, or removing one Scar. The GM considers the whole effect when assigning magnitude, including how many characters it heals and how long it lasts.

Damage and healing normally happen instantly and remain afterward. Each character can benefit from a healing zone once per cast.

### Magic is a team sport

Magic can be a team effort. Separate casts can build on each other.

One character creates fire, another opens a portal, and a third blows the fire through it. The GM prices each cast separately. Each player rolls for their own character. Each character gains Exposure for their own cast.

## Exposure and Mutation

Exposure is a little like radiation poisoning. It collects as your character uses the Arcane Aether, letting them reshape the world while it reshapes them.

Whenever the character reaches **10 Exposure**, they undergo a Mutation. Roll on the [[mutation-tables|Mutation table]]. 

The Mutation permanently occupies an [[Core Mechanics#Inventory and conditions|inventory slot]]. Subtract 10 Exposure and keep any excess.

## Magic Items and Dispersing Exposure
An Arcanist, or caster, can move Exposure out of their body and into an object, creating a magical relic.

A relic:

- Takes one inventory slot.
- Holds up to **10 Exposure**.
- Takes an undisturbed hour to create or refill.
- Has one magical function chosen when it is created.
- Can be created or refilled once per in-game day.

Creating a new relic consumes **2 of the character's current Exposure**. This Exposure is spent, not stored. During the same hour, choose how much additional Exposure to transfer, up to the relic's capacity. Subtract it from the character's current Exposure and record it in the relic.

For example, creating a relic with 4 stored Exposure removes 6 Exposure from the character: 2 to make the object magical and 4 to store. A new relic may start empty. Refilling an existing relic costs only the Exposure transferred, with no creation cost.

Anyone who can operate the relic can activate it. Describe an effect within the relic's function. The GM assigns its Magic Magnitude using the [[Magic System#Magic Magnitude|normal casting guidelines]]. Subtract that magnitude's Exposure cost from the relic's stored Exposure. No casting roll is made, and the character using it gains no Exposure.

The relic cannot produce an activation that costs more Exposure than it currently holds. At zero Exposure, it is inert until an Arcanist refills it. It remains a relic and does not need the creation cost paid again.

Get creative with your magic items. Like a soul jar thats holds more gear, a fork that shoots fire balls, gloves that put people to sleep.

Relics can be traded or sold like other valuable objects.
