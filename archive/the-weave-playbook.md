# THE WEAVE
### A Powered-by-the-Apocalypse game of magic, mutation, and ruin

*Working draft — v0.1 — ⚠️ ARCHIVED (Mana build)*

---

## The Premise

All of reality is held together by a single source of energy: **the Weave.** It is the fabric and the loom both — every stone, every breath, every law of nature is the Weave holding its shape.

A rare few gain access to it. They are **weavers.** A weaver does not learn spells. A weaver reaches into the fabric of the world with their mind and *insists* it change — and the world, sometimes, obeys.

The cost is written into the act. Touching the Weave touches you back. Every reach reshapes the one who reaches. Push too far and you become **untethered** — your mind dissolves into the loom, and what's left wears the shape of a demon, hunted by everyone who survives you.

The world has wizards and witches and spellbooks. They are not weavers. They are scavengers, using relics and anomalies that real weavers left behind. **Weavers are the secret, true source of all magic — and they are dying species, because the power that makes them eats them alive.**

---

## The Core Move: WEAVE

> **When you reach into the Weave to force a change on the world,** picture exactly what you want to happen, describe it, and roll **2d6 + your Mana modifier + any relevant Tags.** (See *Mana-to-Modifier* and *Tags* below.)

There is no spell list. There are no schools. If you can imagine it and the GM agrees the Weave could do it, you may attempt it. You may steal ideas from any storybook, any other game, any myth — the Weave makes all things possible. The one thing it will not grant is a **wish**, because the Weave *is* the wish that made everything, and it cannot wish upon itself.

**Outcomes (2d6 + Mana + Tags):**

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| **Double 6s** | Triumph. The weave takes fully **and** you gain a permanent **Mana slot** with *no* mutation (as long as this cast does not overfill you). Optionally, the GM offers a Spell Tag for the style you just cast. |
| **10+** | Full success. The change happens as you intended. **Gain 3 Mana.** |
| **7–9** | Success with complication. The change happens, but the GM introduces a cost, twist, or echo. **Gain 2 Mana.** |
| **6 or under** | The weave does not take. Nothing happens — or worse, the GM may make a move. |
| **Double 1s** | **Wild Magic.** The weave convulses and tears your entire Mana pool out into the world. The more Mana it took, the worse the eruption. See the Wild Magic section. |

> **The casting cost is not Mana. The cost is the risk of mutation.** You do not spend Mana to cast — you *accumulate* it by succeeding, and accumulation is what damns you.

## MANA — Power and Doom in One Number

You begin with **6 Mana slots,** all empty. Successful weaving fills them.

Your Mana total is your casting stat. As your pool fills, your modifier on the Weave roll climbs — but the same Mana that makes you powerful also fills your slots toward mutation. **You buy accuracy with proximity to ruin.**

### Mana-to-Modifier

| Mana in pool | Modifier to your Weave roll |
|---|---|
| **0** | **−2** |
| **6** | **−1** |
| **12** | **0** |
| **18** | **+1** |
| **24** | **+2** |
| **30+** | **+3** *(cap)* |
| *at Level 5 (5th mutation)* | **+4** *(raised cap)* |

A fresh weaver with an empty pool casts at **2d6 − 2** — mostly failure, real danger. Every 6 Mana banked nudges your modifier up one step — a long, earned climb *and* a steady march toward your doom. Between thresholds, round down (5 Mana still reads as −2; 11 still reads as −1).

The cap holds at **+3** no matter how vast your pool grows. It rises to **+4 only after your 5th mutation** — when you stand one step from being lost, the Weave finally answers you almost perfectly. Even then, a raw 2–3 still misses, and a raw 2 (double 1s) still erupts. **Failure is always possible.**

This creates three zones:

- **Low Mana** — weak or negative casting. You are human, nimble, hideable, and bad at magic. Safe.
- **Mid / Full Mana** — your stable power band. Strong casts, controlled risk.
- **Overfilled (Overcast)** — devastating now, catastrophic soon. See below.

### Overcasting

When a cast would push your Mana past your current slot ceiling, you may **let it.** This is overcasting.

- You **mutate** — advancing to your next level (see Mutation, below).
- Because you were *forced* there in a single violent cast, the mutation rolls **higher on the severity ladder** than a measured one would.
- You become **Sickened** (ongoing −1 to all rolls until you rest and recover).
- **While Sickened and at your ceiling, every further cast forces another mutation.** This is the panic button. Mash it to win the fight; pay for it with your soul.

Overcasting never grants *extra* levels — it just gets you to the next one faster and uglier.

## TAGS — Your Character Is What You Can Do

A weaver isn't a block of attribute scores. A weaver is a handful of **named tags** — short, evocative phrases describing what you're good at and what your magic has become. When a tag clearly applies to what you're attempting, it gives **+1.** Tags are your stats; you evoke them by describing how they fit the fiction.

There are three kinds.

### Background Tags — who you were

You start with **three background tags** drawn from your life before the Weave: *Soldier's Blade, Tavern Brawler, Court Manners, Baker's Hands, Street Whispers* — whatever fits the person you were. Evoke them for **+1** on any mundane action they suit, as often as the fiction allows.

> **You may also burn a background tag to fuel a Weave.** Channel your old life into a cast for a one-time **+1** — and that tag is **gone forever.** You can never evoke it again, for magic or anything else. The baker who keeps reaching for the Weave stops being a baker. Who you were is consumable; every desperate cast spends a little more of the person you used to be.

### Spell Tags — what your magic becomes

You start with **one** spell tag — the weave that came most naturally when the Weave first took you. You earn more whenever you **mutate**, and may be offered one on a **double-6.** A spell tag is a narrow, **player-named** style describing the weave you cast and want to keep casting — *Flame, The Drowned, Rot, Binding, Whispering Glass.* Name it after what you reached for, what you want this weaver's magic to be. It gives **+1** whenever you weave clearly within that style.

Spell tags **stack** — three tags that fit one cast give **+3** — and they apply **on top of your Mana modifier** (so a deep specialist can blow well past the Mana cap on their signature weave). Spell tags are permanent; they do not burn.

### Negative Tags — what the Weave did to you

Each mutation also gives a **negative tag** describing your deformity — *Hulking, Glasseyed, Reeking, Cannot Pass As Human.* When the fiction works against it — sneaking, hiding, moving fast, being trusted — take **−1.** A relevant negative tag cancels against a relevant positive one on the same roll.

## MUTATION — The Only Way to Level Up

There is no XP. There is no other advancement. **You grow stronger by growing closer to doom.**

**Everything runs on sixes.** You start at **Level 1** with **6 Mana slots.** Each mutation is a **level-up** that raises your ceiling by 6: Level 1 = 6 slots, Level 2 = 12, Level 3 = 18, Level 4 = 24, Level 5 = 30, Level 6 = **36 — the untethering.** Six starting slots, six-Mana modifier steps, six mutations, 36 at the end.

You mutate — level up — when your Mana would cross your current ceiling (whether you cross it carefully or blow past it overcasting). Each mutation:

1. **Raises your level**, lifting your Mana ceiling by 6 and your modifier tier with it.
2. Lets you **name a new Spell Tag** for the weave you've been casting — **+1** whenever you weave that style again.
3. Inflicts a permanent **physical change** and a **negative tag** that makes you harder to pass as human (see ladder).
4. **Consumes one of your Injury slots, forever.** The more monstrous you become, the more fragile your flesh.

Because your level *is* your tier, Mana and mutation are one track: to hold a vast pool, you must have mutated your way to the slots that hold it. The only way to gain capacity *without* leveling is a **double-6** — the rare, precious exception that lets a careful weaver edge above their tier without paying in flesh.

Mutations make you **slower** and **more frightening.** Whenever you attempt something those traits work against — sneaking, hiding what you are, moving fast, being trusted, passing as ordinary — take the relevant negative tag as **−1.**

**The Specialist's spiral:** weavers naturally deepen along one axis — the pyromancer who becomes fire, the necromancer who becomes the grave. Specialize and you stack tags fast, becoming unstoppable at one thing as doom closes in.

**The Generalist's grind:** chase **double-6s** to bank capacity and tags across many styles *without mutating.* It is slow (≈1 in 36) and your casting stays weaker — but you stay more human, broad, and hidden. The harder road.

### The Mutation Ladder

Your **level** sets the floor of each mutation's severity. Overcasting, or mutating twice in one day (no cooldown), **bumps it up a tier** — a forced second mutation in a day rolls at **+1 tier.**

| Level | Tier | Example Manifestation |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Mark | Glowing eyes; skin that catches light wrong; a voice with a second voice underneath. |
| **2** | Deformity | A limb warps — too long, jointed wrong, scaled or barked or fused. Clothing hides it, barely. |
| **3** | Transformation | A new limb, a tail, wings that don't work, a mouth where none belongs. No hiding it close up. |
| **4** | Hunger / Sense | A new appetite (heat, fear, blood, silence) or a new sense — and the need to feed or use it. |
| **5** | The Mask Slips | You no longer read as human at a glance. Crowds flee. The hunters know your name now. (Your modifier cap rises to +4 here — most powerful, most damned.) |
| **6** | **UNTETHERED** | At 36 Mana your mind unspools into the loom. The character is lost — becomes a demon, an NPC, hunted. |

## INJURIES, DEATH, AND THE WEAVE BETWEEN

You begin with **6 Injury slots.** (Remember: every mutation eats one forever.)

Injuries come from combat, from harsh mutations, and from **Wild Magic (double 1s).** A failed weave *may* wound you if the fiction bites that way; double 1s **always** do.

### When your last Injury slot fills, your spirit separates from your body.

This is not the end. It is a **change of state** — and a catastrophe, because **the body is what holds your Mana.**

- Your spirit keeps your **level and mutations** — your *capacity* and what the Weave made of you travels with your soul. But your **entire Mana pool stays behind in the body**, a charged battery slowly going cold. You leave your power on the floor with your corpse.
- A bodiless spirit **cannot weave.** With no body to hold or gather Mana, the only thing you can attempt is to **take a body** — your own, or someone else's.
- Your abandoned body begins to stiffen. **Rigor sets in at 4 hours; total at 12 hours.** After that, body and the Mana inside it are lost to you forever.
- **Another weaver may give you one of their own Injury slots** to lend your body more life — buying time before rigor. They lose that slot **forever.** This is the deepest gift one weaver can give another.

**Reclaim your own body in time** and you recover everything — body, level, and full Mana pool. **Possess a new body** and you keep your level but arrive at **zero Mana, casting at −2** — a near-doomed weaver reduced to a fumbling novice, with the *capacity* for great power but none of the charge. You must rebuild your pool from nothing.

> **A new body buys you a terrible grace: for a little while, you look human again.** A possessed vessel begins unmarked. But as you weave and re-accumulate Mana, the Weave remembers what you are — your mutations **bloom back** onto the new flesh, reasserting your monstrousness one cast at a time.

> **A weaver's corpse is a prize.** It is a body full of stored Mana, there for the taking by any weaver who can claim it. The Church harvests the bodies of its burned-out initiates for exactly this — the power still cooling inside them.

### The final choice

When the body cannot be held — at the end of the spirit's road, or at the sixth mutation — the weaver faces the same horizon from two directions:

- **Let go** and join the Weave. Dissolve into the fabric you spent your life pulling at. Peace, and the end of the character.
- **Keep fighting** and stay, transformed — a thing that was a person, now something else, no longer fully tethered to the world.

> **Mutation-doom and injury-death are the same threshold, reached by opposite tracks.** One fills with power, one empties of life. Both end as *untethered.*

## MAGIC ITEMS — The Only Way Down

The only way to *lower* your Mana is to channel it out of yourself and into an object.

- **Crafting an item takes many hours** of ritual work — it cannot be done in a fight.
- You pour Mana into the object, lowering your pool. The item holds the **style of magic you last cast.**
- The item can be **used by you, traded, or sold.** Wizards and witches across the world are, knowingly or not, using weaver-made items like these.
- **Refilling an item in play** requires a **short rest.** If you roll **10+** while doing so, the refill is **free** — the Weave is generous. On less, the item simply takes the Mana you just rolled.

Items are the weaver's pressure valve. But every drop you give away is power you no longer have — and the coward who lives entirely off items is exactly the kind of fake "wizard" your lore despises.

## WILD MAGIC — When the Loom Convulses

On **double 1s,** the weave does not fail quietly. It *erupts* — and it pays for the eruption with **you.**

> **The convulsion tears your *entire* Mana pool out into the world, draining you to zero.** Whatever Mana you were holding at that instant becomes the **severity** of the event. The more power you had banked, the more catastrophic the result.

This is the cruelty at the heart of the Weave: **hoarding power makes you a bigger bomb.** The desperate low-Mana caster produces a small, sad misfire. The maxed-out weaver standing near doom rewrites reality and is left suddenly empty, weak, and human again. Same roll. Wildly different ruin.

**Severity by Mana drained** (use whatever feels right — these are guides):

- **0–4 Mana:** roll **d6** on the table. A flicker of wrongness.
- **5–9 Mana:** roll **d10.** A real and lasting scar.
- **10+ Mana:** roll **d10, take the higher of two,** *and* the GM may combine it with an Injury to you or stack a second entry. A reality-shattering event.

| d10 | Wild Magic *(roughly mild → severe)* |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Misfire.** A harmless burst — sparks, a smell of ozone, every candle in reach gutters out. |
| 2 | **The merry catastrophe.** Something absurd and permanent: it rains upward here, colors swap, the dead nearby sit up and applaud. Roll with it. |
| 3 | **Silence falls.** No sound can be made in this place ever again. Speech, screams — nothing. |
| 4 | **The light dies.** This space falls into permanent, unnatural darkness. Nothing relights it. |
| 5 | **Time pools.** A few seconds here now take minutes — or hours — to pass. Permanently. |
| 6 | **Gravity breaks here forever.** Down becomes a direction this place no longer agrees on. |
| 7 | **The wrong door.** A passage opens to somewhere it should not — and does not close. |
| 8 | **Echo.** The last thing you successfully wove happens again, bigger, and out of your control. |
| 9 | **Flesh remembers.** Every living thing present mutates once, randomly — including you. |
| 10 | **Detonation / a scar in the world.** A blast of raw weave: everyone nearby takes an Injury and may mutate, and the GM invents a lasting, reality-warping anomaly here — a place wizards will one day scavenge. |

Wild magic **almost always changes the physical space forever.** Every old weaver battlefield is a zone of broken physics — which is exactly where the world's fake magicians find their relics.

### Counter-Move: SMOTHER THE CONVULSION

The only way to stop a wild magic event is for **another weaver to pull the expelled Mana into themselves before it erupts** — starving the catastrophe by swallowing its fuel. It is an act of sacrifice, and it is dangerous.

> **When you reach into a wild magic eruption to pull its loose Mana into yourself,** roll **2d6 + your Mana** (capped as normal).
> - **10+** — You seize a chunk of the expelled Mana. Choose how much you grab (up to what the event holds); the event's **severity drops by that amount, one-for-one.** That Mana enters *your* pool, with all the overfill and mutation risk that brings.
> - **7–9** — You grab it, but it's worse than you hoped. The GM picks one: you pull *more* than you intended, or it claws you on the way in (**take 1 Injury**).
> - **6 or under** — The weave lashes you instead. **Take 1 Injury** and pull nothing.

**Multiple weavers can stack.** Each rescuer rolls and each one's haul comes off the severity. Three weavers each pulling 3 can turn a 10-Mana apocalypse into a 1-Mana flicker — but those three are now each carrying that Mana toward their *own* ruin, and any of them may overfill and **mutate** to absorb someone else's disaster.

> **This is why weavers gather in circles.** A lone weaver who rolls double 1s has no one to share the blast — they eat it whole. A coven can smother almost anything, but every smothering spreads the damnation around the circle: more Mana, more mutation, more monstrousness pooling in one place, more reason for the hunters to come. Safety and ruin, the same coin as always.

## BECOMING A WEAVER

No one is born a weaver. There are two ways the Weave gets into a person.

### Initiation (deliberate, and never free)

A weaver can intentionally open a normal person to the Weave. This is not charity — it is a **transaction.** In exchange for access to power, the new weaver pays their maker by handing over some of their own **Injury slots,** permanently. The flow of life runs **upward, from initiate to maker.**

This is how a powerful weaver cheats their own doom. Mutation eats your Injury slots; initiation *replaces* them — with someone else's. A master near the brink stays alive by **converting people and taxing them.** The price is whatever they can demand: a fixed toll at initiation, a negotiated sum, or ongoing tribute extracted as the master nears death. The desperate pay more.

A made weaver is therefore born **in debt,** inside a structure, beneath someone.

### Exposure (accidental, and free)

Survive raw contact with weaving and the Weave takes root in you anyway. Two triggers:

- **Survive lethal weaving** aimed at you and live.
- **Survive a wild magic event.**

No one chose to make you. You owe no one. A **wild weaver** is unindebted, unaffiliated — and hunted by both the world *and* the covens, who see free vitality walking around unclaimed.

> **Every weaver is a contagion.** Use lethal magic and fail to finish the job → you have made a weaver. Roll double 1s in a crowd → the survivors may wake to the Weave. This is why weavers practice restraint: violence and carelessness *manufacture the very thing the world hunts,* each new weaver a beacon that may point back at you. (Smothering a convulsion before it erupts prevents this — pull the weave out before it washes over the crowd, and no one is exposed.)

## CHARACTER CREATION

Build your weaver in this order. Most of it is fiction; the numbers are few and deliberate.

**1. Who were you?** Name the life you led before the Weave, and write **three background tags** from it (*Soldier's Blade, Street Whispers, Court Manners*). These are your mundane competence — and your fuel, if you ever burn one into a cast.

**2. The inciting incident.** Every weaver begins at the moment the Weave entered them — this is your zero-point, fresh and unsteady. Choose how it happened: you **survived lethal weaving,** you **lived through a wild magic event,** or an existing weaver **initiated you** (and you may owe them slots for it). The easy default for a group: **one incident made you all** — the same catastrophe, the same survivors, instant cohesion and a built-in mystery (*who did this to us, and why?*). Or write individual origins and answer the bond in step 5.

**3. Your starting state.** **Zero Mana. Six Mana slots, six Injury slots, all empty.** Choose **one starting spell tag** — the weave that came most naturally when the Weave first took you, your signature from the very first moment. You cast at **2d6 − 2** until you bank power. You are new, and the Weave barely listens. Survive long enough and that changes.

**4. Your drive.** One thing pulling you forward and *toward dangerous casting:* a **goal, a vendetta, or a debt.** This is pure fiction, no bonus — but it should demand things only the Weave can give, or you'll have no reason to risk your soul. Make at least one PC's drive impossible to satisfy without weaving.

**5. Your ties.** Blades-style fiction, no dice attached:
- A small **bank of contacts** you can call on — a lord, a smuggler, a priest. Each call is a favor owed.
- **Someone who wants you to fail** — an enemy seed.
- **Why the group is together** — answered by the shared incident, or written here.

**6. Your mentor (shared by the whole party).** Weavers usually learn from one who survived longer. Your group shares **one experienced weaver NPC** who teaches you the ropes — and serves the table as an in-fiction guide to the rules. But longevity in this world is never clean. Set their dials together:
- **How marked are they?** Hideously mutated and hiding it, or eerily intact (the long generalist game)?
- **Why are they really helping?** Genuine mercy — or grooming you as future tribute?
- **What do they carry?** A debt, a secret, a coven they answer to, a vendetta of their own.

A mentor is a warm tutorial figure *and* a loaded gun. They will likely be consumed by the Weave during the campaign — the person who taught you the rules, eaten by them in front of you, leaving you their items, their contacts, and their unfinished business.



Somewhere at the center of the world sits a weaver who has solved the equation the rest die to.

He is, by now, almost certainly the **most mutated being alive** — barely human beneath the robes. By every rule he should have been lost to doom long ago, and he should be hunted on sight. He is neither, because he built an institution to make both impossible.

**The Church is his funnel.** A faith, a hierarchy, a stream of devoted ordinary people — and every one of them a candidate for initiation. He opens them to the Weave and taxes them for Injury slots, harvesting their lifespan to extend his own past every toll his mutations exact. His followers mutate, burn out, and are hunted; he endures on their accumulated slots. The species is not dying out *evenly* — it is being **strip-mined into one man.** He is the reason weavers are vanishing.

**The item trade is his valve, his fortune, and his disguise.** Carrying a doom-sized Mana pool, he constantly bleeds Mana into objects — staving off the mutation ledge, growing rich, and flooding the world with relics. Those relics are what the world's "wizards" and "witches" scavenge and call magic. **He is the secret source of nearly all magic in the world,** and almost no one knows his name.

His monstrousness, which should mark him for the hunters, is reframed by the Church as **holy** — the sacred disfigurement of a prophet, hidden behind hood and mask and doctrine. The institution is camouflage for the monster. What should damn him instead sanctifies him.

### His Goal

He seeks to become a **god.** To rule over his followers not as a prophet but as a deity, and — at the height of his ambition — to seize and **control the Weave itself,** to become the hand on the loom rather than a thread within it.

This is the thing every other weaver's death warns against: he intends to reach the **sixth mutation and beyond** and *not* dissolve into an untethered demon — to walk through the doorway that unmakes everyone else and emerge still himself, enthroned. The entire Church exists to buy him enough life, enough Mana, and enough power to attempt it. Whether the Weave *can* be ruled, or whether the attempt simply ends the world, not even he knows.

### Player Hooks

- **The harvested.** The players are his initiates — in debt, burning out to feed him. The campaign is escape from the pyramid, and that means cutting the funnel at its source.
- **The wild.** The players are exposure-born, unindebted, free — and the Church hunts them as both rivals and unclaimed vitality.
- **The schism.** The players are highly placed within the Church and begin to see what the prophet truly is, and what his ascension will cost the world.

---



- **Set scale by fiction, not by list.** A candle's flame is trivial and may need no roll. A wall of fire is a Weave. Unmaking a city is a Weave with a price you name *out loud, up front* before the player commits.
- **The cap is load-bearing.** Power plateaus at +3 (+4 only at the brink, the 5th mutation); doom never plateaus. The most powerful weaver at your table should be one bad roll from oblivion, and should feel it. And because failure stays possible on a raw 2–3, the dice can always betray them.
- **Reward the brave; let the cowards be safe and small.** Item-hoarders who never mutate are *meant* to be weak. Don't punish them with rules — punish them with the fiction. They are scavengers, not weavers.
- **This is a short arc by design.** A character realistically takes 3–4 mutations across a campaign before reaching the horizon. The game is the brief, incandescent life of a weaver. Tell the table that going in.

## PARKING LOT (ideas considered, not adopted)

- **Dice-pool casting.** Instead of a flat modifier, Mana could grant extra d6s (every 6 Mana = +1 die), rolling the pool and keeping the best two. Elegant — "collect dice" becomes literal, and wild magic naturally becomes a young weaver's danger (a big pool almost never shows double 1s on its best two). *Shelved* because it forces redefining double-6s/double-1s, retuning wild-magic odds, and reworking how tags add in. The flat 6-per-step modifier solves the actual playtest problem (tight Mana targets) with one number and leaves everything else untouched. Revisit only if the flat version feels stale in play.
