# Core Rules
### The core idea
**The dice:** 
 - Common action **2d6 + stat**. 
 - Casting action **2d6 + mutation level + spell tag**
 - On a failure you gain **Luck Point** that allows the player to push the story. 
 - When performing magic, or casting,  you collect **Exposure** like radiation poisoning. 
 - The more you use it the more you pay. 

 The rest is role play and imagination.


## ♦️Stats
These stats are how good or skilled a character would be at a mundane or non-magical action.

- Armor: 
- Harm: 
- Scars:

- **Strength:** Might, muscle
- **Dexterity:** speed, stealth, precision
- **Willpower:** Endurance, resolve
- **Charm:** Persuasion, presence

*Max range −3 to +3.*

## 🎬 Taking an action
State what your characters intent is for an action then roll 2 six sided dice and add appropriate stat, to see the level of success. 

| Total         | Result                                            |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **6 or less** | **Failure.** It goes wrong. **Earn 1 Luck Point** |
| **7 to 9**    | **Partial success with complication.**            |
| **10+**       | **Full success.** You do it clean.                |

### 🎲 Special rolls

**Double 6s are a critical success.**
  - In combat: 
  Your attempted action is a marvelous success and it does **double your damage**.
  - Casting: the spell succeeds **and** you take a new [[Spell Tags|spell tag]], or grow an existing ones effectiveness (+2, +3…).

**Double 1s: critical failure.**
  - In combat: a total failure. Take **1 Luck and 1 harm or your weapon is damaged **.
  - Casting: roll on the [[wild-magic-table|wild magic table]]. You 
  - Your attempt to cast has gone horribly wrong.
  - ⚠️ A critical fail can be bought down for **3 Luck**. 

**Any other doubles earn a luck point.** 

## 🍀 Luck Points

You earn **1 Luck every time you:**
- Fail a roll, 6 or less.
- Roll doubles over double 1s.
- Earn **2 Luck** when your character completes a goal.

**Note:** you must accept the failure to take the luck point. 

### Spend Luck:
- Push a **failure** up to a **partial success**
- Push a **partial** up to a **full success**
- **Assist a teammate:** say what you do to help, then **roll a d6 yourself** and add it to their total. *(The assist die only adds; it can't make doubles.)*
- Take an **extra turn**
- **3 Luck:** downgrade a **critical fail** to a normal failure, then push from there if you can afford it
- **5 Luck:** raise one stat by 1

## 📏 Distance as Zones
This game generalizes distance from the characters as zones instead of exact feet or squares.

| Zone      | What it means                                    |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Close** | Melee. You can touch it.                         |
| **Near**  | Across the room. One move to close.              |
| **Far**   | Two moves to reach. Bowshot.                     |
| **Long**  | Somewhere else. Getting there takes travel time. |
- **One move** shifts you one zone. Moving and acting in the same turn is fine unless something is stopping you.
- **Weapons have a reach:** melee works at Close, thrown at Near, bows and crossbows at Far.

## ⚔️ Combat
Characters gets 2 action points they can spend on either moving in space or taking an action.
- Declare an action, then roll to see the effect
- **Damage** is the weapon's rating. 
- Armor subtracts

## 🪄 Casting

> [!quote] Casting bonus = your [[mutation-tables|mutations]] + one [[Spell Tags|spell tag]] that fits.

**Three steps, every time:**

1. **The caster says what they want:** how big, how far, how long.
2. **The GM names the price in Exposure.**
3. **The caster rolls 2d6 + casting bonus, or dials it back.**

The price is always known before the dice. Nobody gets damned by surprise.

### What it costs

**Start at 1. Add for reach. Add for duration.**

| Reach: how much of the world it touches    |        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------ |
| One person or thing, **Close** or **Near** | **+0** |
| A room, a handful of people, or **Far**    | **+1** |
| A building, a street, or **Long**          | **+2** |

| Duration: how long it stays        |             |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Instant, or it ends with the scene | **+0**      |
| A few hours                        | **+1**      |
| A day or more                      | **+2**      |
| A year or more                     | **+3**      |
| **Forever**                        | **5 total** |

**Nothing costs more than 5.** If the dials add past it, it's a 5.

The casters should know the number of Exposure before they roll.

### 🚨 How damage does a cast cause

Not every cast does damage. The ones that do work like a weapon: **the GM says 1, 2, or 3** for how hard it lands, the same scale as a sword.

| Damage | The cast                                                                                                |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1**  | A lash, a shove, a grasping hand.                                                                       |
| **2**  | A wall of fire, a thrown spear of ice.                                                                  |
| **3**  | The building comes down on them.                                                                        |
| **5**  | **Legendary.** Only a 5-Exposure reaches this, and only with a [[mutation-tables\|mutation]] behind it. |

## Exposure & mutation

> [!danger] Every 10 Exposure → **mutate**
> - Roll on the [[mutation-tables|d66 mutation table]] and take what comes.
> - Mark a **new spell tag** based on what you just cast.
> - Your Exposure levels reset. Excess exposer can carry over if that makes sense.

### 🧙‍♂️ Magic Items
Magic items are the only way to **reduce Resonance Exposure**

**Forge a new item:**** An Attuner can spend a few hours in focused concentration to imbue a physical object with a specific magical effect. The GM and player determine its Exposure cost using the normal casting rules. This becomes the item's charge cost.

**Recharge:** An Attuner can recharge a magic item with a few minutes of focused concentration, transferring enough Exposure to restore its charges.
