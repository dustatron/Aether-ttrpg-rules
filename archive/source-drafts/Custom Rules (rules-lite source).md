# THE WEAVE
### A rules-light game of magic, corruption, and the price of power

*Working draft — v0.1. The light build: 2d6, one roll, play to find out.*

---

## The Premise

All of reality is held together by a single source of energy: **the Weave.** A rare few can reach into it and *insist* the world change — and sometimes it obeys. But the Weave marks those who use it. Bend reality and it bends you. Reach too far and you become **untethered** — lost, monstrous, hunted.

The world has wizards and witches and enchanted relics. They are scavengers. The relics they use were all made by real weavers, who bleed their own corruption into objects to survive their power. **Weavers are the secret, true source of all magic — and it is killing them.**

You play a weaver. This is the story of your brief, incandescent life.

---

## The Core Roll

Everything you attempt — fighting, sneaking, persuading, noticing, *and* weaving — uses **2d6.** Use two different colors: one **Hope** die, one **Fear** die.

Roll 2d6 **+ the relevant stat, or (for casting) your casting bonus**, against a **difficulty the GM sets by how hard the task is:**

| Difficulty | Target |
|---|---|
| **Easy** — routine | **6** |
| **Medium** — real effort (the default) | **8** |
| **Hard** — a serious challenge | **10** |
| **Very Hard** — few succeed | **12** |
| **Legendary** — beyond skill | **14** *(see below)* |

**Legendary actions are special.** They require the Weave's attention:
- You can only *attempt* a Legendary weave if you have **at least 1 mutation** — the Weave won't listen to the still-pristine. The impossible is reserved for those it has already begun to claim.
- On a Legendary attempt, the only crit is **double 6s** — an automatic triumph, the Weave answering beyond your station. Other doubles don't crit at this tier; resolve them normally against the 14.

*(Legendary sits high on purpose: with only their stats, a normal person basically can't reach it — but a weaver whose casting bonus has grown through mutations and stacked tags can. The Weave is how you punch past human limits.)*

Read the result two ways at once — **did it work,** and **what did it cost:**

**Meet or beat the target = success. Under it = failure.** Then the higher die colors it:

| | **Hope die higher** | **Fear die higher** |
|---|---|---|
| **Success** | **Yes** — clean. Gain 1 Hope. | **Yes, but** — it works, with a complication. GM gains a Fear token. |
| **Failure** | **No, but** — you miss, with a small consolation. Gain 1 Hope. | **No, and** — you miss, and it gets worse. GM gains a Fear token. |

So **"yes, but" (partial success) = a success rolled with Fear high.** That's your bargain: you got what you wanted, and it cost something.

**Two patterns override the table:**
- **Doubles (2–6)** → **critical success.** "Yes, and" — you succeed and something extra happens. *(Casting only: on **double 6s**, gain a spell tag with no mutation — or, if a tag already fits, it deepens instead: +2, then +3, and so on. On non-casting rolls, doubles are still "yes, and" but grant no tag.)*
- **Double 1s** → **critical fail.** "No, and" — you fail and something bad happens. *(Casting only: roll on the wild magic table. On non-casting rolls, it's just a bad miss.)*

*(On a tie between the dice, the player takes the Hope.)*

**What applies to which rolls:**
- **The Hope/Fear die** (gain Hope on a high Hope die; GM banks a Fear token on a high Fear die) — **every roll**, mundane or magic.
- **Corruption** (+1 on a successful raw weave) — **casting only.** Swinging a sword or picking a lock never corrupts you.
- **Double 6s → a spell tag** and **double 1s → wild magic** — **casting only.** On mundane rolls, doubles and snake eyes still crit up or down, just without tags or wild magic.

One roll: difficulty decides success, the Hope/Fear die decides the cost and who gains a currency.

## Casting (the WEAVE move)

> **When you reach into the Weave to force a change on the world,** describe exactly what you want, and roll **2d6 + your casting stat + spell tag (if any).**

There's no spell list. If you can picture it and the GM agrees the Weave could do it, attempt it — steal from any myth or story. The one thing the Weave won't grant is a **wish**, because the Weave *is* the wish that made everything. The GM sets the difficulty by scale: lighting a candle may be Easy or need no roll; a wall of fire is Medium; unmaking a city is Legendary, with a price named up front.

**A successful raw cast gives you +1 Corruption.** The Weave listened — so it marked you. (Failed casts don't corrupt; the fabric simply ignored you.)

Two ways to cast *without* corruption:
- **Through a magic item** (spends one of the item's charges — see Items).
- The GM may rule some trivial effects need no roll and no cost.

---

## CORRUPTION — Power and Doom in One Number

Corruption is the heart of the game. It is **both your fuel and your ruin.**

- You **gain** it by successfully casting raw (+1 per success).
- You **spend** it to forge magic items and to work permanent changes on the world.
- **Every 5 Corruption you don't spend, you MUTATE** (see below). Five mutations. The **6th crossing makes you a monster** — untethered, lost, gone.

So you are a furnace: doom pours in every time your magic works, and your only relief is to forge that doom into objects before it tips you over. Stop creating, and you transform.

---

## MUTATION — What Corruption Makes You

Each mutation (every 5 Corruption) is a permanent step:

1. **+1 to your casting stat.** The Weave answers you more readily as you become less human. *(This is the only way casting gets stronger.)*
2. **Name a new spell tag** for the magic you've been working — **+1** whenever you weave that style.
3. Gain a permanent **mutation** — a physical change and a **scar tag** (see below). Roll your mutation level's table, or decide what fits the story.

| # | The change |
|---|---|
| 1 | A mark — glowing eyes, small claws, sharpened teeth. Hideable under a hood. |
| 2 | A deformity — a limb warps; clothing hides it, barely. |
| 3 | A transformation — a new limb, a tail, a mouth that shouldn't be. |
| 4 | A hunger or a new sense — visible on the body, with a need that comes with it. |
| 5 | The mask slips — you no longer pass as human. The hunters know you. |
| 6 | **MONSTER** — you are lost to the Weave. The character becomes an NPC. |

**Scar tags** are the downside of mutation: when the fiction works against your monstrousness (hiding, being trusted, moving quietly), take **−1.**

---

## SPELL TAGS — What You're Good At

A spell tag is a magic action you've become good at through practice — *Flame, The Drowned, Rot, Binding.* When your weave fits the tag, add **+1** to your casting roll. Tags stack (different tags that all fit add together).

You **start with one** (your signature — the magic that came to you in your inciting incident). You gain more by **mutating** and by **rolling double 6s on a cast.** Rolling double 6s in a style you *already* have a tag for deepens it instead: that tag climbs to +2, then +3, and so on — the specialist's reward for casting the same magic again and again.

---

## MAGIC ITEMS — The Safe Conduit

Weaving through an item costs you no Corruption — the item carries the toll instead. This is how weavers survive their own power, and why the world is full of their relics.

- **To forge an item, spend Corruption** (takes time — hours of work, not a combat action). The Corruption you pour in becomes the item's **charges.** A 3-Corruption item holds 3 corruption-free casts.
- The item casts the **style of magic you last wove** into it. Use it, trade it, or sell it.
- **Spent items** can be recharged by pouring in more Corruption.

This is your furnace valve: raw casting fills you with Corruption; forging items bleeds it back out into the world. You're always choosing — cast raw and take the mark, or cast safely through an item you bled to make.

---

## HOPE — Your Luck and Your Growth

The Hope die feeds you **Hope** (gain 1 whenever the Hope die is higher — on a success *or* a miss). Cap **5.** Spend Hope to:
- **Push a roll** — turn a *no* into a *yes*, or upgrade an outcome one step.
- **Grow** — bank it; **5 Hope raises a stat** (or earns a new move, in downtime).

Hope is the reward for boldness — you gain it most by rolling, risking, and reaching. Every point is a choice: spend it now to save yourself, or hold it to become more. And in a game about slow doom, spending your last Hope means something.

---

## THE FEAR TOKEN — The GM's Currency

When the Fear die is higher, the **GM banks a Fear token.** The GM spends tokens to:
- introduce a complication, twist, or cost,
- bring in a threat or hunter,
- make a hard move against the players,
- make the Weave itself convulse.

The players' own risky magic *fuels the danger they face.* This is the engine of play-to-find-out: nobody, not even the GM, knows what the Fear tokens will buy.

---

## DEATH — Hearts, Lives, and Wounds

Damage is fast and forgiving; death is slow and permanent.

- You have a pool of **Hearts** (start with **5**) — your health. Weapons and dangers deal harm equal to their rating — a *2-harm* sword fills 2 Hearts on a hit. **Hearts heal** between fights.
- **Fill your Hearts and you lose a life:** take a **Wound** (permanent), and your Hearts reset.
- **Wounds are the death clock.** At the start of a campaign, the GM sets **how many Wounds until death** — the **deadliness dial.** Default is **3.** Gritty and lethal: 2. Epic saga: 5–6.
- **Armor** blocks any hit of equal or lower harm. A hit *greater* than your armor gets through — reduce your armor by 1 instead of taking the harm. Repair or replace it later.

### When your last Wound falls — spirit separation

Your spirit leaves your body. But **your body holds your power** — your Corruption and your capacity stay with the flesh.
- A bodiless spirit **cannot weave.** It can only try to **reclaim its body** or **possess another.**
- The body stiffens: **rigor at 4 hours, gone at 12.** Reclaim it in time and you're whole again.
- Possess a **new body** and you keep your tags and level but start fresh — and as you weave, your mutations **bloom back** onto the new flesh.
- A weaver's **corpse is a prize** — a body full of power, there for any weaver to claim.

## CHARACTER CREATION (five minutes)

1. **A concept** — one evocative phrase for who you were. *Disgraced knight. Grieving midwife. Smuggler with a debt.*
2. **Stats** — you have four mundane stats. Distribute **+2, +1, +1, 0** across them:
   - **Force** — violence, strength, breaking and enduring.
   - **Grace** — speed, stealth, precision, acting under pressure.
   - **Wits** — perception, knowledge, cunning, reading a situation.
   - **Heart** — presence, persuasion, deception, will. *(As you mutate, your scar tags erode Heart — the more monstrous you become, the harder it is to be human.)*
3. **Your signature spell tag** — the magic that came to you in your inciting incident. The shape the Weave first took in you.
4. **Your inciting incident** — how the Weave got in: you survived lethal weaving, lived through a wild magic event, or a weaver initiated you. *(Easy group option: one event made you all.)* This is also where your signature tag comes from.
5. **Your drive** — a goal, vendetta, or debt that will make you keep casting even as it damns you.
6. **Your starting state** — 0 Corruption, 5 Hearts, 0 Hope, 0 Wounds (death at 3), 0 mutations, one spell tag.

> **Casting is not a stat you buy.** Your casting bonus = your **number of mutations + any spell tag** that fits the weave. A fresh weaver casts at **+1 in their signature style** (the one tag) and **+0 in anything else** — shaky, wild, dangerous. Magic only grows by mutating (+1 each) and gaining more tags. Force, Grace, Wits, and Heart are for the *mundane* world; the Weave is its own thing, powered by what it's turning you into.

Everything else — your mutations, your tags, your monstrous shape — you discover in play.

---

## PLAY TO FIND OUT

The GM doesn't plan the story; they set a situation and let the dice and the players' choices reveal it. The Fear tokens surprise everyone. The corruption creeps. The mutations arrive unbidden. Nobody at the table knows how a weaver's story ends until it does — in monstrousness, in death, or in the quiet horror of surviving.
