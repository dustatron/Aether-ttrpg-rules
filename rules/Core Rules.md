# Core Rules

The Arcane Aether is a rules-light game about ordinary people who can reshape reality and must live with the cost.

For the premise and reading order, start with [[Start Here]]. To make a character, use [[Character Creation]].

## The Dice

Say what your character intends to do. **Roll 2d6 plus the fitting stat.** Casting uses one relevant Spell Tag instead.

Roll when the outcome is uncertain and failure would change the situation. Otherwise, let it happen or tell the player what it costs in time or resources.

| Total | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 6 or less | Failure. It goes wrong. Resolve the failure, then gain 1 Luck. |
| 7 to 9 | Partial success. Your character does it, but there is a complication. |
| 10 or greater | Full success. Your character achieves what they intended. |
| Double 1s | Critical failure. |
| Double 6s | Critical success. |

Check doubles on the two dice that determine the result. A critical result replaces the normal result.

A complication should move events forward. Your character might lose something, end up in danger, attract attention, or get hurt. A partial success still accomplishes what you rolled for.

## Luck

Each character can hold up to **10 Luck**.

Gain **1 Luck** after you accept and resolve a failed roll. Gain **2 Luck** when your character achieves a Key Goal and you cross it off their list.

You may give your Luck to another player whenever you like.

### Spending Luck

| Cost | Buy |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Add one die to your roll and keep the highest two. |
| 1 | Assist another character. |
| 2 | Play a flashback that can affect the present. |
| 5 | Advance your character. |

### Add a die

Spend 1 Luck before resolving a roll. Roll **3d6 and keep the highest two**. Only the kept dice count for double 1s or double 6s.

### Assist another character

Spend 1 Luck and explain how your character helps. The active player adds 1d6 to their roll and keeps the highest two dice.

If the roll fails, only the active player gains 1 Luck. Everyone who assisted shares the consequences of a failure or partial success.

### Flashbacks

Spend 2 Luck to play a short scene from your character's past that affects the present. Maybe they bribed the kitchen servant to leave the back door unlocked.

Play the scene and roll if its outcome is uncertain. Then return to the present with the result. Costs and consequences apply normally. In a turn, the flashback counts as your character's action.

### Advancement

Spend 5 Luck to choose one:

- Increase a stat by 1, to a maximum of +3.
- Increase a Spell Tag by 1, to a maximum of +3.
- Increase maximum Harm by 1.
- Gain an Archetype Perk.

## Magical Casting

Your character can command the patterns of Aether and reshape reality through thought and imagination.

1. Describe the effect: its reach or size, what it does, and how long it lasts.
2. The GM sets the Exposure cost using the casting dials.
3. Accept the cost or reduce the effect, then roll 2d6 plus one relevant Spell Tag.

Casting follows the normal roll results, with these additional effects:

- **Failure:** The cast fails and costs no Exposure. Resolve the failure, then gain 1 Luck.
- **Partial success:** The cast works. Mark the agreed Exposure and suffer a complication.
- **Full success:** The cast works. Mark the agreed Exposure and add a beneficial flourish beyond the intended effect.
- **Double 1s:** The cast fails and costs no Exposure. Roll on the [[wild-magic-table|Wild Magic table]], adding the character's current Exposure.
- **Double 6s:** The cast works. Mark the agreed Exposure, then gain a new Spell Tag related to the cast or increase the Tag used by 1, to a maximum of +3.

A beneficial flourish cannot add damage, reach, size, or duration that was not included in the Exposure cost.

### Spell Tags

Spell Tags represent what your character has learned through casting. Fire, invisibility, healing, ice, mind reading, floating, talking to animals, light, pain, and poison are all possible Tags.

Choose one relevant Spell Tag for each cast and add its rating to the roll. Tags do not stack, run out, reduce Exposure, or limit what your character may attempt. Without a relevant Tag, roll 2d6 with no bonus.

When your character gains a Mutation through casting or you roll double 6s on a cast, gain a new +1 Tag related to the cast or increase the Tag used by 1, to a maximum of +3. If both happen on the same cast, gain this benefit only once.

The [[Spell Tags]] table is a prompt sheet, not a spell list.

### Casting Limits

Mutations allow your character's body to withstand more Exposure. Their safe limit for one cast is:

**3 plus their number of Mutations.**

You may have your character cast above this limit. On a success, mark 1 Fatigue for each point of Exposure above the limit, in addition to marking the Exposure.

The GM tells you the full cost before you roll.

### A Spell's Exposure Cost

**Exposure = Reach or Size + Force and Effect + Duration.**

Every cast costs at least **1 Exposure**. A harmless, instant effect at touching range, such as creating a spark of flame, costs 1.

| Reach or Size | Add |
| --- | --- |
| One creature or object your character touches | 1 |
| A medium room, a handful of people, or a wagon-sized object | 2 |
| Across a field, a building, a street, or a field of targets | 3 |
| Barely in sight or beyond it | 4 |

Use the highest Reach or Size cost. Do not add reach and size together. For a transformation, price the larger form.

| Force and Effect | Add |
| --- | --- |
| A change without forceful impact, such as a spark, transformation, invisibility, or gentle floating | 0 |
| A firm shove, deal 1 damage, or restore 1 Harm | 1 |
| A hard throw, deal 2 damage, or restore 2 Harm | 2 |
| A crushing impact, deal 3 damage, restore 3 Harm, or heal one Scar | 3 |

| Duration | Add |
| --- | --- |
| Instant, or until the end of the current scene | 0 |
| A few hours, up to a day | 1 |
| More than a day, up to a month | 2 |
| More than a month, up to a year | 3 |
| Years, but less than a lifetime | 4 |
| A lifetime or forever | 5 |

Magical damage is the amount bought with Exposure. It is not doubled or tripled by the casting result.

Casting effects end when their paid Duration expires. A portal closes, a transformed person returns to their previous form, invisibility ends, and a flying boat comes back down.

Damage and healing are instant and remain afterward. Magic may start a fire, but the fire continues only while it has fuel.

## Exposure and Mutation

Exposure is a little like radiation poisoning. It collects as your character uses the Arcane Aether, letting them reshape the world while it reshapes them.

Whenever the character reaches **10 Exposure**, they undergo a Mutation. Roll on the [[mutation-tables|Mutation table]]. The Mutation permanently occupies an inventory slot. Subtract 10 Exposure and keep any excess.

If a cast also causes Fatigue, resolve its Exposure and any resulting Mutations before marking the Fatigue.

## Magic Items

An Arcanist can move Exposure out of their body and into an object, creating a magical relic.

A relic:

- Takes one inventory slot.
- Holds up to **6 Exposure**.
- Takes an undisturbed hour to create or refill.
- Has one magical function chosen when it is created.

During that hour, choose how much Exposure to transfer, up to the relic's capacity. Subtract it from the character's current Exposure and record it in the relic.

Anyone who can operate the relic can activate it. Choose the reach or size, force and effect, and duration within the relic's function. Price the activation using the normal casting dials and subtract that cost from the relic's stored Exposure. No casting roll is made, and the user gains no Exposure.

The relic cannot produce an activation that costs more Exposure than it currently holds. At zero Exposure, it is an ordinary object until an Arcanist refills it.

This is why powerful Arcanists leave so many relics behind. They cannot keep all their Exposure in their bodies, and they cannot carry every object they fill.

## Inventory and Conditions

Your character has **12 inventory slots**. Gear fills from the top down. Conditions fill from the bottom up, taking the lowest available slot.

An item usually takes one slot. Small things of the same kind bundle together, such as a handful of arrows, spikes, or three rations. Worn armor does not take a slot.

Mutations, Scars, and Fatigue are conditions. If one takes a slot holding an item, that item is dropped.

When conditions claim all twelve slots, the character is lost. The final condition determines what happens in [[#Death and Dying|Death and Dying]].

## Movement

A move shifts your character one zone toward or away from something. Skip their action to take a second move.

| Zone | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Close | Melee. Close enough to touch. |
| Near | Across the room. One move to Close. |
| Far | Two moves from Close. Bowshot. |

Walls, locked doors, crowds, and deep holes still matter.

## Combat

Characters act first, then enemies. The players roll 1d6. The highest roll goes first, then play moves clockwise around the table. If the characters are surprised, the attackers act first.

### Your Character's Turn

Your character can take **one move and one action** in either order. They can attack, cast, catch their breath, use something, assist someone, or try something else.

Roll **2d6 plus Strength** for hand-to-hand combat or **2d6 plus Dexterity** for a ranged attack. Your character must be able to reach the target with what they are using.

| Result | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Failure | The attack fails and the situation gets worse. |
| Partial success | Deal the weapon's damage and suffer a complication. |
| Full success | Deal double the weapon's damage. |
| Double 6s | Deal triple damage and ignore Armor. |
| Double 1s | The attack fails. The character takes 1 damage or their weapon breaks, whichever fits. |

On a failure or partial success, an enemy may hurt your character, push them back, damage their gear, or create another fitting complication. The enemy still takes its regular turn.

### Damage and Armor

Weapons deal damage based on their size and weight:

- Small or concealable: **1 damage**.
- Normal: **2 damage**.
- Heavy or two-handed: **3 damage**.

Apply damage added by a Perk before doubling or tripling the attack.

Armor reduces incoming damage. Worn armor and shields stack to a maximum of **3 Armor**, although Legendary creatures may have more.

When your character takes damage, subtract their Armor. If the damage exceeds the Armor, apply the remaining damage and the Armor breaks. If the damage does not exceed the Armor, apply no damage and leave the Armor intact.

A critical success ignores Armor without breaking it. Broken Armor must be repaired by someone with the necessary skills. Pay them or owe them a favor.

### When Enemies Attack

Use the enemy's Threat from [[Monsters]]. The GM rolls **2d6 plus Threat**.

| Total | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 6 or less | The attack misses. |
| 7 to 9 | Deal the enemy's listed damage. |
| 10 or greater | Deal the enemy's listed damage and cause a complication. |
| Double 6s | Deal triple damage and ignore Armor. |
| Double 1s | The attack fails and the enemy suffers a mishap. |

A crowd attacking one target rolls once, using its highest Threat and best single damage. Its numbers can still block exits, surround a character, or create other complications.

## Harm Scars and Recovery

Subtract damage from your character's current Harm. Their Archetype sets the maximum. Harm cannot fall below zero.

When a hit brings the character to zero Harm, mark one Scar. Each damaging hit taken at zero Harm causes another Scar. A single hit causes only one Scar, regardless of excess damage.

Each Scar occupies an inventory slot until removed. Healing Harm does not remove Scars.

### Healing

- **Catch their breath:** Spend the character's action and roll 1d6. Restore that much Harm. They cannot do this while Fatigued.
- **A couple of quiet hours:** Roll 2d6 and restore that much Harm if the character has no Fatigue.
- **A full night's rest:** Remove one Fatigue. If none remains afterward, restore all Harm.

Harm cannot exceed the character's maximum. Fatigue prevents all Harm recovery, including magical healing.

Removing a Scar requires magic or about a week of care from a healer. Removing it frees its inventory slot.

## Death and Dying

The condition that claims your character's final inventory slot determines their fate.

| Final condition | Ending |
| --- | --- |
| Mutation | The character becomes a Demon. The Aether has changed them beyond themselves. |
| Scar | The character's body dies. Their spirit passes on, or lingers because of unfinished business. |
| Fatigue | The character is taken by the Aether. They have nothing left to hold it back. |

The character becomes part of the world's lore, and the player makes someone new. A lingering spirit may still matter, but the character's playable life is over.
