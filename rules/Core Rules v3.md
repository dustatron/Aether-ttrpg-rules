# The Goals
1. Less is more
	1. Rules light 
	2. Mash up between PbtA and OSR inspired stats an inventory.
2. A system where progress happens in play and not explicitly an after game down time action.
3. Magic is free form and as much a gift as it is a curse
4. Balanced encounters warp the game. Not everything should be hard but killable. 
5. Powerful but never safe
6. Fast paced with strong motivation for the group to work together and advance together
7. Explore and discover the world like a hexcrawl
8. Simple enough structure the game can be improvised
9. Low Prep

## The Dice rolling

State what your character’s intent and action. Roll **2d6 add the fitting stat to get the total**.

| <br>Total     | Result                                                                                |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **6 or less** | **Failure.** It goes wrong. Gain 1 Luck.                                              |
| **7 to 9**    | **Partial success.** Your character does it, but there is a complication.<br><br><br> |
| **10+**       | **Full success.** Your character masterfully achieves their objectives.               |

**A partial deals full damage. 
But the effect may hurt the player or damage their weapon.

- **Cast and you gain Mana Exposure points.** At 10 exposure points you mutate, and the mutation takes an inventory slot.


## Combat

Combat works similarly to other rolls. 

Roll 2d6 + strength for hand hand, combat, or Dexterity for arranged attacks, like shooting an arrow.

See the casting section for when you want to attempt a offensive magical attack.

Your weapons will do the damage they have listed.

Rolling a standard success with consequence is a standard amount of damage. 

Rolling a full success is double damage.

### Double 6s

Rolling double 6s is a critical success. It ignores armor and does triple damage.

When taking harm the character marks that harm against their harm score. When they run out of harm, they take a scar which takes an inventory slot. 

### Double 1s
This is a critical fail. 
The character takes harm or breaks their weapon.

### Healing

Harm points can be regained relatively quickly. If I character takes a moment from battle and skips their turn as their action they can roll one day six and regain that much harm. 

Taking a Couple hours in the story to rest and not be under stress should regain 2d6 harm.

A full nights rest would replenish their harm fully.

Scars need magical healing or Help from a healer and about a week of healing time.

## Luck Points

**Earn 1 Luck** when you fail a roll.
**Earn 2 Luck** when your character crosses a goal off their list.

### Luck caps at 10

Anything over is lost.

**Characters can share luck to anyone at the table, whenever you like.**

### Spend it in play

| Cost  | Buy                                                         |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | Push a **failure** up to a **partial success**.             |
| **2** | Push a **partial** up to a **full success**.                |
| **1** | **Assist a teammate.** Roll a d6 and add it to their total. |
| **3** | Downgrade a **critical fail** to a normal failure.          |

Say how your character assists another and then toll 1d6 and add it to their total.

### Spend on upgrades

5 luck points can be spent at any time to upgrade a stat or improve the effect of a spell tag by 1.
to a maximum of **+3**.

6 luck to take **Another perk from your archetype.**

You can give luck to other players to allow them a stat increase as well.


## Magical Casting
**Three steps to casting:**

1. **The caster says what they want to achieve:** The distance from themselves, how long the effect lasts, level of damage.
2. **The GM declares the amount of Mana Exposure based on the amount of desired effect**: recommended cost dials below.
3. **If the caster is fine with the cost they rolls 2d6 + spell tags, or they dials back the effect to pay a lower cost.**

The price of the desired effect should be known before the caster rolls.

Once the dice land the narrative should be effected. 

**A failed cast should probably not cause mana exposure.**
 
The player should take a luck point and the story should advance in some way.

The failed cast could cause a snow storm or people to shrink to the size of mice for a hour.

Take the failed cast as a way to inject weirdness into the scene.

Rolling double ones is a critical fail while casting and causes a role on the wild magic table. Add the number of mana exposure the character has to this wild magic roll.


### Mana Mutations

A character with no mutations can only handle a total of 3 mana exposure point per cast. 

If a player wants to attempt something larger than what they can handle, they can attempt it but it will cause harm from too much mana at once. 

The harm caused is at the discretion of the players and the GM when the roll happens. But you might consider having the character take a fatigue in an Inventory slot. 

Every mutation increases that characters’ resistance to Mana exposure. Allowing them to cause larger changes in the fabric of time and space without taking any harm to themselves. 

Each mutation is their spell casting level. They can handle their level plus the initial 3.

### Mana Exposure 
Mana exposure is similar to radiation poisoning.

The more you are exposed to it the more that collects in your body. 

Casting is powerful and reshapes the world but it is warping your character and stealing your essence. Eventually turning you into a monster.

Collect Mana Exposure every time you cast successfully. As you mutate you develop resistance to the effects of the exposure and then the character is able to successfully make larger casting effects without making themselves sick.

### Casting effect  v Exposure Amounts 
**Start at 1. Add more based on size and scale.**

The smallest successful cast should be at least 1 exposure.

Use the three Ds of Mana exposure
Distance, Durations, and Damage to figure out the price.

### Spell tags

Spell tags can be spent as a bonus to a roll or to reduce exposure level of a casting effect. 

Your character gains a tag when they mutated based on the cast they were performing that caused them to mutate.

Or when rolling double 6s when casting.

Your spell tag can get an additional +1, if you were using it to cast when you mutate or roll double 6s.

Example tags would be… 
Invisibility, fire, mind reading, floating, healing, ice, earth, wind, animals talking, light, pain, poison. 


## Movement
Movement in this game should be kept simple and generalized. Character and object distances can be identified by zones listed below. 

| Zone           | What it means                                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Close**      | Melee. You can touch it without taking a step.   |
| **Near**       | Across the room. One move to close.              |
| **Far**        | Two moves. Bowshot.                              |
| **Long range** | Somewhere else. Getting there takes travel time. |

**One move shifts you one zone.**

## Language
There are other languages in this world. If you wanted your character to read or understand a conversation of a language  they don’t know, that would require a casting that sustains over time. This would be at least 2 corruption. 


## Inventory
You have 12 inventory slots.
They will be used to carry your weapons and treasures. 
They will also be taken over by Mutations, scars, and fatigue. 
Common inventory items will fill down and Conditions like mutations will fill up. 
If an item is in that slot when you take a scar or mutation you lose that item. 

Mutations permanently block a slot.
Scars can be healed by way of magic or finding a healer in the story and spending the time in the story to repair the scar.

## Conditions
Fatigue or scars are conditions a character takes because actions didn’t go as they planned. These elements should take a slot in one’s inventory. If there’s an item already in that slot, they lose that element that. 

A condition is something that can be healed or otherwise removed through rest or a quest. 

When a player has fatigue they can no longer regain Harm quickly, I simply skipping their turn and taking a quick rest. They can only regain Harm after relieving their fatigue. 


Fatigue can be relieved one at a time by taking a full nights rest.


# Character Creation
## Archetypes
This game has class like character templates called Archetypes. 

But your character development is not on a defined track you need to stay in. 

These setups are simply there as a jumping off point, intended as an inspiration for your character’s earlier like before they became exposed to the arcane resonance and the true nature of the resonance started shifting them into what will soon unfold.

These Archetypes give your character starting gear, stats, and an initial life you used to have. 

Your character may fight the temptation to use their new abilities and not give in to its corrupting powers. Instead following the path of their origins. I say do that if it’s how you want to play your character. 

Just know that being able to reshape the world at will would be nibbling in the back of your character’s mind. They could heal a friend, save a village from an impossible problem, protect the children from the monster that hunts them. It would be so easy with the arcane power waiting at their fingertips.

## Death and dying
What character death in this game takes a couple forms. When mutations fill the inventory, they become a demon like monster and lose themselves Completely. 

That character now is Lost and is now apart of the lore and legends of the world.
The player should likely roll I knew character

If the character fills their inventory with scars their body dies. Their spirit passes to the spirit round unless the player desires to keep the spirit around because of unfinished business.

If the mutations are 50% of the inventory and the scars are the other 50% of the inventory, the character becomes an undead monster with wild magic unpredictability.
