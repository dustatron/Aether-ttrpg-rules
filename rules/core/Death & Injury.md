# Death — Hearts, Lives, and Wounds

> [!abstract] The shape of it
> Damage is **fast and forgiving**; death is **slow and permanent.**

## Hearts (health)

- You have a pool of **Hearts** — start with **5**.
- Weapons and dangers deal harm equal to their rating: a *2-harm* sword fills **2 Hearts** on a hit.
- **Hearts heal** between fights.

## Armor

- **Armor blocks any hit of equal or lower harm.**
- A hit *greater* than your armor gets through — reduce your armor by **1** instead of taking the harm. Repair or replace it later.

## Wounds (the death clock)

- **Fill your Hearts and you lose a life:** take a **Wound** (permanent), and your Hearts reset.
- **Wounds are the death clock.** At campaign start the GM sets the **deadliness dial** — how many Wounds until death.

| Tone | Wounds to death |
|---|---|
| Gritty & lethal | **2** |
| **Default** | **3** |
| Epic saga | **5–6** |

---

## When your last Wound falls — spirit separation

Your spirit leaves your body. But **your body holds your power** — your [[Casting & Corruption|Corruption]] and your capacity stay with the flesh.

- A **bodiless spirit cannot weave.** It can only try to **reclaim its body** or **possess another.**
- The body stiffens: **rigor at 4 hours, gone at 12.** Reclaim it in time and you're whole again.
- **Possess a new body** and you keep your tags and level but start fresh — and as you weave, your [[Mutation|mutations]] **bloom back** onto the new flesh.

> [!warning] A weaver's corpse is a prize
> A body full of stored power, there for any weaver to claim. The Church harvests the bodies of its burned-out initiates for exactly this. See [[The Church & The Prophet]].
