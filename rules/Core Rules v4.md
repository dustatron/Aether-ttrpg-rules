# Core Rules v4

The arcane resonance and the weirdness below.

In this game the idea is every character can cast, though not everyone will.

Magic is freeform. It is only limited by what a character can imagine and how much Exposure their body can handle. Borrow a spell idea from any game you like.

Your character could heal a friend, save a village from an impossible problem, protect the children from the monster that hunts them. It would be so easy with the arcane power waiting at your character’s fingertips. But is your character willing to pay the price?

This is a rules-light mix of Powered by the Apocalypse (PbtA) outcomes and old-school play. A character's stats only tell a little of who they are. Build out the rest through your ideas and the goals you have for them.

Not everything needs a roll. Maybe your character knows how to speak Orc because they spent a summer lost in an Orc camp pretending to be a baby Orc's doll. That sounds good to me.

Let the story guide the ruling. Tell the players what danger their characters can see before asking them to commit to an action.

## Making a character

[[Character Creation v2|Character Creation]]. 

## The Dice

Say what your character intends to do. **Roll 2d6 + the fitting stat.** Casting uses a spell tag instead.

Roll when the outcome is uncertain and failure would change the situation. Otherwise, let it happen or tell the player what it costs in time or resources.

| Total | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 6 or less | Failure. It goes wrong. Accept the failure and gain 1 Luck. |
| 7 to 9 | Partial success. Your character does it, but there is a complication. |
| 10+ | Full success. Your character achieves what they set out to do. |

A complication follows from what is happening: the character makes noise, loses something, ends up in danger, or gets hurt. A partial success still gives you what you rolled for.

**Any two 1s are a critical failure. Any two 6s are a critical success.** Count assist dice and ignore the total when checking. A critical replaces the normal result. Bonuses do not create doubles, but Luck can change the result.

## Luck Points
**1 Luck:** Accept a failed roll to take the Luck. You can spend this luck at the end of your turn.
**2 Luck:** When your character achieves a key goal and you cross it off their list**.
Luck caps at **10 per character.** 
Give your Luck to anyone at the table whenever you like.

### Spend Your Luck

| Cost | Buy |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Assist a teammate. Commit before the roll. |
| 2 | Play a flashback that can affect the present. |
| 2 | Raise a dice result by one level. |
| 3 | Downgrade a critical failure to a normal failure. |
| 5 | Raise a stat or spell tag by 1, to a maximum of +3. |
| 6 | Raise maximum Harm by 1 or take an archetype perk. |

Spend Luck to change a result after the dice are rolled but before resolving it. 
Luck can change a result only once. 

### Assist a Teammate

Any number of players can assist. Before the roll, each says how their character helps, spends 1 Luck, and rolls 1d6 alongside the active player's 2d6. Add every die and the active player's bonus.

If the final result is a failure, only the player making the original roll earns 1 Luck. On a failure or partial success, every involved character shares the consequences. The GM describes how each is affected.

Enemies do not earn or spend Luck.

### Flashbacks

**2 Luck**: Play out a short scene from the character's past that affects the present. Maybe they asked the kitchen servant to leave the back door unlocked.

Play the scene and roll normally, then return to the present with the result. Costs and consequences still apply, and accepting a failure earns 1 Luck as usual. This stands in for the players action. 

## Magical casting

Say what you want the Arcane Resonance to do.

1. **The player describes the effect their character wants:** what changes, how far away it is, how much it affects, and how long it lasts.
2. **The GM names the Exposure cost** using the dials below.
3. **The player accepts the cost or dials back the effect**, then rolls 2d6 plus a relevant spell tag.

On 7+, the spell works and you mark the agreed Exposure. On 7 to 9, it also brings a complication. On 6 or less, it fails and costs no Exposure. Accept the failure to gain 1 Luck.

On double 1s, roll on the [[wild-magic-table|wild magic table]] and add your character’s current Exposure.

Double 6s make the spell work and grant or improve a tag, as described below.

For a damaging spell, a partial deals the damage you bought with Exposure, a full success doubles it, and double 6s triple it. Armor does not reduce spell damage.

### Spell tags

A tag is something your character’s magic has learned to do: fire, invisibility, healing, ice, mind reading, floating, talking to animals, light, pain, or poison.

Choose **one relevant tag** for a cast. Its rating is the bonus to your roll, up to **+3**. Without one, roll 2d6. Tags are not consumed, do not stack, and never reduce Exposure.

When your character mutates from casting or you roll double 6s on a cast, take one new **+1 tag** connected to that spell, or improve the tag you used by 1, to a maximum of +3. If both happen on the same cast, take this benefit once.

### Mutations and casting limits

Mutations let the character’s body handle more Exposure. Their safe limit is **3 + their number of mutations**, to a maximum of **10 Exposure per cast**.

You can have your character cast above their safe limit. If it succeeds, mark **one fatigue for each Exposure point above the limit**, as well as the Exposure itself. The GM tells you how many slots that will cost before you roll. No cast can cost more than 10 Exposure; reduce its scope if necessary.

### Pricing a spell's Exposure

**Exposure = 1 + Reach / Size + Force and Effect + Duration.**

Start with 1, then add a cost from each table. The base cost covers a simple transformation. Pay Exposure only on a successful cast.

| Reach / Size | Add |
| --- | --- |
| One person or smaller creature or object, by touch | +0 |
| Across a room, a handful of people, or a wagon-sized object | +1 |
| Across a field, a building, a street, or a field of targets | +2 |
| Barely in sight or beyond it | +3 |

Use the highest Reach / Size cost. Do not add reach and size together. For a transformation, price the larger form.

| Force and Effect: how hard the spell pushes, hits, or heals | Add |
| --- | --- |
| A change without a forceful impact: transform something, make it invisible, or float it gently | +0 |
| A firm shove, deal 1 damage, or restore 1 Harm | +1 |
| A hard throw, deal 2 damage, or restore 2 Harm | +2 |
| A crushing impact, deal 3 damage, restore 3 Harm, or heal one scar | +3 |

| Duration: how long the magic lasts | Add |
| --- | --- |
| Instant, or up to the end of the current scene | +0 |
| A few hours, up to a day | +1 |
| More than a day, up to a month | +2 |
| More than a month, up to a year | +3 |
| Years, but less than a lifetime | +4 |
| A lifetime or forever | +5 |

Force and Effect is one dial, so a damaging shove pays once. Add the costs of separate benefits, such as restoring Harm and healing a scar.

Transformations and invisibility end after their paid Duration. A transformed target then returns to its previous form.

Damage and healing are instant and remain afterward. A fire can keep burning with fuel. Healing cannot exceed maximum Harm or multiply on a full or critical success. Healing a scar returns its slot.

Wards, a creature's nature, or a Legendary's weakness may require something first. Tell the player what their character needs to discover or do.


### A few examples

**Invisibility by touch for a few hours:** 1 base + 1 Duration = **2 Exposure**.

**Throw a chair across the room for 2 damage:** 1 base + 1 Reach / Size + 2 Force and Effect = **4 Exposure**.

**Heal one scar by touch:** 1 base + 3 Force and Effect = **4 Exposure**.

## Exposure and mutation

Exposure is a little like radiation poisoning. It collects as a character uses the Arcane Resonance, letting them reshape the world while it reshapes them.

**When the character’s Exposure reaches 10, they mutate.** Roll on the [[mutation-tables|mutation table]]. The mutation permanently takes an inventory slot. Subtract 10 Exposure and keep any excess.

If the cast also causes fatigue, resolve its Exposure and any mutation first, then its fatigue.

## Magic Items

Your character can move Exposure out of their body and into an object, making a magical relic.

This is why powerful magic users leave so many relics behind. They cannot keep all their Exposure in their bodies, and they cannot carry every object they fill.

A relic takes **one inventory slot**, holds up to **6 Exposure**, and takes **an undisturbed hour** to fill or refill. Subtract the transferred Exposure from the character's current total. Scars and mutations remain.

See [[Magic Items]] for shaping a relic, its charges, and using it.

## Inventory and conditions

Your character has **12 inventory slots**. Gear fills from the top down. Conditions fill from the bottom up, taking the lowest available slot.

An item usually takes one slot. Small things of the same kind bundle together: a handful of arrows, spikes, or three rations. Worn armor is free, as in [[Character Creation v2|Character Creation]].

Mutations, scars, and fatigue are conditions. If one takes a slot holding an item, that item is dropped. Fatigue can also come from exhaustion or a fitting consequence.

**The character's playable life ends when conditions claim all twelve slots.**

## Movement

Keep distances simple. Describe where things are, then use these zones when the distance matters.

| Zone | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Close | Melee. Your character can touch it without taking a step. |
| Near | Across the room. One move to Close. |
| Far | Two moves. Bowshot. |
| Long range | Somewhere else. Getting there takes travel time. |

One move shifts your character one zone toward or away from something. Walls, locked doors, and deep holes still matter.

## Combat

### Who goes first

Characters act first, then enemies. Players choose their order each round. If one side is surprised, the other takes one turn before regular rounds begin.

### Your character’s turn

Your character takes **one move and one action**, in either order. They can attack, cast, catch their breath, use something, help, or try something else.

Roll **2d6 + Strength** for hand-to-hand combat or **2d6 + Dexterity** for a ranged attack. Your character needs to be able to reach the target with what they are using.

| Result | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Failure | The attack fails and the situation gets worse. |
| Partial success | Deal the weapon’s damage and suffer a complication. |
| Full success | Deal double the weapon’s damage. |
| Double 6s | Deal triple damage and ignore armor. |
| Double 1s | The attack fails. The character takes 1 damage or their weapon breaks, whichever fits. |

On a failure or partial, an enemy might hurt your character, push them back, or damage their gear. The enemy still takes its regular turn.

### Damage and armor

Weapons deal **1 damage** if small and concealable, **2** if normal, or **3** if heavy and two-handed. Apply perk damage before doubling or tripling. Record the total with the weapon.

Armor subtracts from each hit, to a minimum of zero damage. Worn armor and shields stack to a maximum of **3 armor**. Legendary creatures may have more.

Multiply damage first, then subtract armor. If a noncritical hit exceeds the armor, apply the remaining damage, then break the shield first. If there is no shield, break the worn armor. A hit no greater than the armor deals no damage and leaves it intact. A critical ignores armor without breaking it.

Mending armor requires someone who knows how. Pay them or owe them a favor.

### When enemies attack

Choose the enemy's Threat from [[Monsters]]. The GM rolls **2d6 + Threat**.

| Total | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 6 or less | It misses. |
| 7 to 9 | It deals its listed damage. |
| 10+ | It deals its listed damage and causes another complication. |

Double 6s deal triple damage and ignore armor. On double 1s, the attack fails and the enemy suffers a mishap.

A crowd attacking one target rolls once, using its highest Threat and best single damage. Its numbers can still block exits, surround a character, and make things worse.

### Morale and Fleeing

Most living creatures want to survive and will not fight to the death.

When a creature's remaining Harm is at or below half its maximum, roll **2d6 + Threat**.

| Total     | Result                                                                                         |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 6 or less | It flees, surrenders, or takes a bargain.                                                      |
| 7 to 9    | It changes what it is doing: gives ground, calls for help, or goes after what it really wants. |
| 10+       | It holds.                                                                                      |

Mindless, driven, or bound creatures may never check morale. 

The GM may call for another morale roll after the creature takes more damage.

## Harm, scars, and recovery

**Subtract damage from your character’s current Harm.** Their archetype sets the maximum. Harm stops at zero.

When a hit brings the character to zero Harm, mark **one scar**. Each further damaging hit at zero gives them another. A hit gives only one scar, regardless of excess damage. The character stays on their feet unless the fiction or their final slot says otherwise.

Each scar takes an inventory slot. Healing Harm does not remove it.

### Healing

- **Catch their breath:** spend the character’s action and roll 1d6. Restore that much Harm. They can do this during a fight if they have no fatigue.
- **A couple of quiet hours:** restore 2d6 Harm if the character has no fatigue.
- **A full night’s rest:** remove one fatigue from the character’s sheet. If none remains afterward, restore all their Harm.

Harm cannot exceed the character’s maximum. Fatigue blocks all Harm recovery, including magic.

Removing a scar requires magic, or a healer and about a week. It returns the slot. Ordinary rest does not.

## Death and dying

The condition that claims the final slot decides what happens.

| Final condition | Ending |
| --- | --- |
| Mutation | The character becomes a demon-like monster. The Resonance has changed them beyond themselves. |
| Scar | The character’s body dies. Their spirit passes on, or lingers because of unfinished business. |
| Fatigue | The character is taken by the Resonance. They have nothing left to hold it back. |

What fills the other eleven slots does not matter. The character becomes part of the world's lore, and the player makes someone new. A lingering spirit may still matter, but the character's playable life is over.
